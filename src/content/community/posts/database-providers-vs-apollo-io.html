My team is comparing Apollo against buying a list outright. We are a small sales team, mostly running one or two campaigns a quarter. Which way should we go?

The choice is not really about data quality — it is about whether your team uses contact data daily or occasionally, because the two models price for completely different usage patterns. Apollo is a sales engagement platform with a database attached. Access is metered by credits and seats, and the value comes from SDRs living in the tool — prospecting, enriching, sequencing and dialling inside one workflow. If your reps are in a contact database every day, that integration is worth paying for, and a one-time file will feel clumsy by comparison. On the pattern you describe, that is the case where Apollo genuinely wins. Where it stops fitting is exactly your situation. One or two campaigns a quarter means you are paying a seat and credit structure across the months where nobody opens the tool. Credits expire on their schedule, not yours. And when the subscription ends, continued access to the data ends with it — what you keep is what you exported while you were paying. Database Providers works the other way. You specify a segment, we compile and verify the file at order time, and you own it outright. No seat licence, no credit balance, no renewal date, and the file does not stop working when you stop paying. For a team running campaigns in bursts, that maps to how the spend actually occurs. The honest trade-off: you do not get a sequencing tool, a dialler, or live enrichment inside your CRM. You get a dataset, delivered as CSV or Excel with headers matched to your destination system. If you need the workflow layer, you need a platform — and you should buy one rather than trying to make a file do that job. The other difference worth checking before you decide is coverage in your specific segment. Platform databases are broad but uneven, and depth varies sharply by vertical and geography. Ask both of us for a count and a sample on your exact target segment, run them through your own verification tool, and compare the results rather than the marketing claims. That comparison answers the question better than any feature table. Apollo's pricing, credit policy and contract terms are published on their own site and change over time — check them directly rather than relying on a third-party summary, including ours.

Compliance note for healthcare outreach in the US: CAN-SPAM governs the send, but if your messaging touches patient data or clinical claims, FDA promotional rules and your own regulatory review apply on top. Physician contact data is business contact information and is not protected health information, so HIPAA does not apply to the list itself — but it does apply to anything a recipient sends back to you.
