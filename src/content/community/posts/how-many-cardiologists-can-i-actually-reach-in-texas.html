The gap between that number and the licensed cardiologist population in Texas is worth understanding, because it changes how you segment. A large share of cardiologists work inside hospital systems where the practice email is gatekept and never appears in any commercial dataset. Many practise under a group entity rather than their own name. Some hold multiple affiliations, which produces duplicates that have to be resolved down to one record. What remains after all of that is the reachable set — and it is always smaller than the licence roll. For a cardiac imaging product specifically, the practical move is to segment on practice affiliation rather than title alone. A cardiologist in an independent or small-group practice has meaningfully more purchasing influence over imaging equipment than one inside a large hospital system, where the decision sits with a procurement committee and a radiology director. Filtering on that split typically produces a smaller list that performs better, and it usually means adding hospital administrators and radiology leads as a second segment rather than trying to reach the physician directly.
